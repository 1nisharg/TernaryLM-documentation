Abstract

Large language models demand substantial compute, limiting deployment on edge and resource-constrained hardware. We present TernaryLM, a 132M-parameter decoder-only transformer trained natively with ternary weights {−1, 0, +1} (log23 ≈ 1.58 bits/weight) via a straight-through estimator and learnable per-layer scaling, rather than quantizing a pretrained full-precision model post hoc. Trained on 12.9M TinyStories tokens on a single NVIDIA L40S GPU in 1.3 GPU-hours, TernaryLM reaches a validation perplexity of 5.52. An architecturally identical FP32 twin trained with the same data and schedule fits the training set more closely (train perplexity 2.43 vs. 3.35) but overfits, ending at 5.97 and never dropping below 5.58 at any epoch. Under identical settings, the ternary constraint narrows the train/validation gap (1.65× vs. 2.46×) and improves held-out perplexity, consistent with regularization rather than underfitting (single seed). Exported with int8 ternary codes and one FP32 scale per projection, TernaryLM needs 1.93× less storage (272.6 MB vs. 527.4 MB; 2.58× with 5-per-byte packing) and 5.2× less peak inference memory than FP32 inference, at a 1.37× latency cost on standard kernels. Under a protocol-matched frozen-backbone probe, TernaryLM matches FP32 GLUE transfer to within 0.5 points, with both limited by the narrow pretraining corpus. Per-layer zero-state occupancy is near-uniform (39–41%) and is almost perfectly explained by weight-distribution shape (r = 0.97 with kurtosis), while latent weights progressively concentrate at the quantization threshold. Every result comes from logged single-GPU runs, showing that controlled low-bit language-model research is within reach of modest compute budgets.

Method

Every attention and MLP projection keeps a latent FP32 weight \(W\) and its own learnable scale \(\alpha > 0\). On every forward pass, \(W\) is re-quantized with a per-layer threshold:

$$W_q = \alpha \cdot \mathrm{sign}_\tau(W), \qquad \mathrm{sign}_\tau(x) = \begin{cases} +1 & x > \tau \\ 0 & |x| \le \tau \\ -1 & x < -\tau \end{cases}, \qquad \tau = 0.5 \cdot \mathrm{std}(W)$$

The backward pass uses the straight-through estimator, \(\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial W \approx \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial W_q\), and \(\alpha\) receives its exact gradient. The untied token embedding and output projection stay in FP32. The FP32 twin used for every comparison is identical, except that its projections are ordinary full-precision linear layers. It uses the same data, data order, schedule and seed.

Regularization, not underfitting

A small train/validation gap alone cannot tell regularization apart from underfitting. What does is held-out performance. The FP32 twin fits the training data better, but its validation perplexity bottoms out at 5.58 in epoch 9 and then rises by 6.9%. TernaryLM keeps improving and finishes lower. From epoch 10 onward it beats the FP32 twin at every epoch, including the twin's best epoch.

Efficiency, correctly accounted

For inference, each ternary projection is stored as an int8 tensor of codes plus its FP32 α. The weight α·t is materialized inside each layer and fed to a standard GEMM, with no custom kernel. The embedding and output projection (35.6% of parameters) stay FP32, so storage shrinks 1.93×, not the 4× a byte-per-weight count over the whole model would suggest.

What the ternary weights look like

Zero-state occupancy tracks weight shape

Every block maps 39.4–41.0% of its weights to zero, just above the 38.3% that a Gaussian matrix would give under the same threshold. The small excess is almost fully explained by each layer's weight shape: occupancy correlates with the excess kurtosis of its latent weights at r = 0.97. With a per-layer threshold, occupancy therefore measures distribution shape, not sensitivity to quantization.

Latent weights pile up at the threshold

During training, the latent weights do not collapse onto three clusters. Instead, sharp peaks form exactly at ±τ. The share of weights within ±0.05 std of the threshold nearly doubles, from 7.5% to 13.6% (Gaussian: about 7%). This is consistent with straight-through updates oscillating around the decision boundary.

Limitations

  • One seed per model, on one narrow, synthetic corpus (TinyStories), at 132M parameters and 12.9M training tokens.
  • The FP32 twin shares the ternary model's regularization settings and was not tuned separately.
  • Frozen-probe GLUE transfer is weak for both precisions.
  • No matched BitNet b1.58 (absmean) baseline and no ablations of the design choices.
  • Storage is unpacked, and latency is higher than FP32 without ternary kernels.
Relevance to resource-constrained research. Each run took 1.1–1.3 hours on a single GPU. Controlled low-bit language-model experiments like these are reproducible without industrial compute.
